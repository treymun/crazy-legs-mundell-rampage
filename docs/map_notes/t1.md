# T1 SHAPES: notes for the guide (pass 16)

What the geometry engine can say since pass 16, for T10 to fold into
MAP_AUTHORING.md. Everything here is in a level's `geometry` block
(`src/level_geometry.gd`); plans write it through `tools/levels/geo_kit.gd`,
map sources through their objects (`tools/map/map_source.gd`).

## Geometry version 2

A block is written as `"version": 2` only when it uses something version 1
lacks (`LevelGeometry.version_for`): terrain, a raked wall (`z1b` not equal
to `z1`), or a slab not walked on steeper than 1. Every older level is
written and read exactly as before. A version 2 block also draws a sloped
slab's sides as true quads (rakes) instead of strips.

## Raked walls (gables, lean-to walls, arch haunches)

A segment's top runs from `z1` at `a` to `z1b` at `b`. It collides, stops
looks and shots along its sloping top, and is drawn as one quad.

```json
{"id": "gable_w", "a": [10, 6], "b": [10, 9], "z0": 2.4, "z1": 2.4, "z1b": 4.2,
 "thick": .2, "tile": "barn"}
```

In a plan: `Geo.raked(b, "gable_w", Vector2(10, 6), Vector2(10, 9), 2.4, 2.4, 4.2, {"thick": .2, "tile": "barn"})`.
In a map source: a `wall` with `"z1b": 4.2` (no openings: split a raked wall
where its openings are; the validator says `raked_openings`).

## Terrain (hills, ditches, banks, creek beds, cliffs)

A patch is corner heights in whole centimetres over a rectangle of cells,
row by row from the north-west corner ((w + 1) * (h + 1) of them). Each cell
is two triangles split north-west to south-east. A face steeper than 1 in 1
is a cliff: it stops a body and is drawn upright in the `cliff` tile; a
patch's edge standing over lower ground is drawn down to it in the same
tile.

```json
"terrain": [{"id": "knob", "x": 4, "y": 4, "w": 2, "h": 2,
  "heights": [0, 0, 0,  0, 80, 0,  0, 0, 0],
  "tile": "grass", "cliff": "quarry_face",
  "palette": ["grass", "dirt"], "rows": ["01", "10"]}]
```

- `tile`: the patch's floor (the cells' own otherwise); `palette` + `rows`
  a letter a cell (`0`-`9` then `a`-`z` index the palette, `.` the patch's
  tile); `light`: the patch's light (each cell's own otherwise).
- A later patch over the same cells wins.

In a plan:

```gdscript
Geo.hill(b, "knob", [4, 4, 20, 20], 3.0, {"tile": "grass"})   # a round hill, 3 high
var bank := func(x: float, _y: float) -> float: return 2.0 if x >= 28 else 0.0
Geo.terrain(b, "bank", 24, 4, 10, 8, bank, {"tile": "gravel"})  # any heights: a Callable or a list
Geo.lift_onto_terrain(b)   # last: pickups, hands and boards onto the surface
```

`Geo.terrain` paints the patch's tile into its cells and sets their ground
to the patch's lowest corner (so nothing reading a cell's ground sticks out
of the hill, and the grid draws no steps under it).

How it plays:
- the surface is the ground: bodies stand on it, spawn on it, walk up and
  down it (no step limit on a smooth slope up to 1 in 1);
- a rise of more than a step under a body's middle, or a cliff within its
  reach, stops it; walking off a cliff's top falls;
- sight and shots with heights: a point under the surface is solid (a hill's
  crest hides what is behind it). A look without heights has the surface for
  its floor (as on a deck), so only a sheer cliff right at it can stop it:
  ask with heights;
- the nav graph: a cell's node stands on the surface at its middle; a walk
  up a cliff or a patch's high edge is no walk.

## Slopes not walked on

A slab with `"walk": false` may slope up to 4 in 1 (76 degrees) in a version
2 block (a steep roof, a bank's face): it collides as a wedge, nobody stands
on it. In a source: a `ramp` with `"walk": false`.

## Cut lines

A deck, block or ceiling may be cut by up to 8 lines (an octagonal gazebo
floor). The engine and the view always took any number.

## Import (`map.gd -- import`)

- A face sloping up 45 to 76 degrees comes in as a `ramp` with `walk` false.
- An upright face whose top slopes (a gable end) comes in as raked walls
  along it (one a stretch between its corners), when it has no hole and a
  level foot. A plain wall, or one with openings, comes in as before.
- Still refused (`skip_unsupported` leaves them out): a wall leaning over, a
  face steeper than 76 degrees that is not upright, a face leaning down.

## Refusals (one line each, the level does not load the piece)

| what | the line |
| --- | --- |
| terrain in a version 1 block | `geometry: terrain needs version 2` |
| a patch not an object | `terrain: a patch is not an object` |
| x, y, w, h not whole | `<id>: x, y, w and h are whole cells` |
| a side under 1 or over 256 | `<id>: a patch is 1 to 256 cells a side` |
| past the map's edge | `<id>: the patch runs off the map` |
| wrong number of heights | `<id>: heights wants N corners ((w + 1) * (h + 1))` |
| a height not whole, or past 100 m | `<id>: heights are whole centimetres` / `a height past 10000 cm` |
| a tile that does not exist | `<id>: no tile named <name>` |
| rows the wrong size or letter | `<id>: rows wants N lines` / `each row is N letters` / `row R letter L is not in the palette` |
| a walked slope over 1 (or any over 1 in version 1) | `<id>: slope steeper than 1` |
| a face not walked on over 4 | `<id>: slope steeper than 4` |
| a raked source wall with openings | `raked_openings` |

## Limits that stay (said plainly)

- A solid grid cell is full height: a building to stand on is geometry.
- The cell roof is one height.
- Hands climb steps and ladders, not slabs higher than a step (nor cliffs).
- A look asked without heights is the flat test (and ignores the terrain's
  rise).
- Things set down on terrain do not roll down it (Tumble's slope reads
  slabs); marks lie flat on it.
