# T5 KIT: notes for the guide (pass 16)

Whole buildings and bits in one line each, for T10 to fold into
MAP_AUTHORING.md and for T7 (sources) and T8 (player maps) to compile
through. The kit is `godot/src/map_kit/` (typed game code, so it runs in the
game as well as in the tools); its numbers, styles and every piece's options
are `godot/data/tuning/map_kit.json`.

## One piece, one line

A piece is a small dictionary. Only `piece` and `id` are needed; everything
else has a default chosen to look right.

```json
{"piece": "farmhouse", "id": "home", "x": 10, "y": 6}
{"piece": "barn", "id": "barn", "x": 30, "y": 4, "face": "e", "style": "barn_red"}
{"piece": "fence", "id": "front_rail", "x": 8, "y": 18, "w": 14, "kind": "picket", "gate": 7}
```

The keys every piece takes:

| key | default | what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `piece` | (needed) | the piece's name (below); `type` is read as a synonym |
| `id` | (needed) | letters, digits and `_`; unique on the map. Every part made is named `<id>_<part>` (`home_wall_s_3`, `home_roof_a`) so a review can name it |
| `x`, `y` | 0 | the footprint's north-west corner, whole cells |
| `w`, `d` | the piece's | the footprint's width (local x) and depth (local y), whole cells, before turning |
| `face` | `s` | the side the front faces: `s`, `w`, `n`, `e` |
| `turn` | 0 | quarter turns clockwise (0 front south, 1 west, 2 north, 3 east); overrides `face` |
| `mirror` | false | flips the piece left to right before turning (stairs on the other side, the chimney at the other end) |
| `style` | the piece's | a material set (below) |
| `variant` | 0 | 0: picked by dice from the id and the map's seed; 1 to 3: that variant of the style |
| `area` | 0 | the district (number) its cells and parts belong to |

A turned piece's footprint on the map is `w` x `d` (front south or north) or
`d` x `w` (front west or east), its north-west corner at (`x`, `y`). A piece
builds inside its footprint; only roof overhangs (up to 0.4), a farmhouse's
chimney (0.35) and a silo's cage hoops stick out a little.

## The pieces

Sizes are `default [smallest..largest]`. Cost is pieces of geometry at the
smallest / default / largest size (a map's budget counts these: 1,200 a map,
320 a piece). Walk-ins and floors are what the kit check walks to.

## Styles and variants

| style | walls | roof | trim | floor | foundation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `barn_red` | barn | tin | kit_trim | kit_porch | kit_stone |
| `whitewash` | kit_clapboard | kit_shingle | kit_trim | kit_porch | kit_stone |
| `grey` | kit_clapboard_grey | tin | kit_trim | porch | cinder |
| `plank` | wood | tin | trim_eave | porch | dirt |
| `tin` | tin | tin | trim_eave | concrete | concrete |
| `brick` | brick | kit_shingle | kit_trim | floor_tile | kit_stone |
| `cinder` | cinder | tin | trim_eave | concrete | concrete |
| `trailer` | trailer | tin | kit_trim | floor_tile | cinder |
| `staves` | kit_staves | tin | trim_eave | concrete | concrete |

Each style has up to three variants (a shingle roof for tin, grey boards,
a bayou shack's planks, ...). `variant` 0 lets the piece's id and the map's
seed pick one, so a row of houses of one style is not all alike; the same
map always picks the same.

## For a versus map

- Every building has doorways on two or three sides (barns, stores, gas
  stations and churches three or four), so nobody is trapped.
- Windows are real holes 0.8 wide from 0.9 to 1.7 over each floor: you see and
  shoot through them; the walls (0.2 thick) stop both.
- Cover inside: partitions, counters, shelves, pews, hay bales, a dock, knee
  walls on the stand. Rails, wire and picket fences do not stop shots.
- Height to fight from: the farmhouse's and store's roofs (ladders), the
  balcony over the porch, the gas station's roof and canopy, the barn's loft,
  the church's belfry, the silo's platform, the water tower's balcony, the
  hunting stand, catwalks.
- Upper-floor doorways (the balcony, the hay door, the water tank) are open
  doorways for now: each is in its piece's `doors` with `hung: false`, and
  its `z`, `width` and `head`, so T4's doors can hang there.

## Using it

In a plan (`tools/levels/<id>_plan.gd`):

```gdscript
const Geo := preload("res://tools/levels/geo_kit.gd")
var parts := Geo.kit(b, [
	{"piece": "farmhouse", "id": "home", "x": 10, "y": 6},
	{"piece": "barn", "id": "barn", "x": 26, "y": 4, "face": "w"},
	{"piece": "fence", "id": "rail", "x": 8, "y": 20, "w": 30, "gate": 12},
], 1234)  # the map's seed: looks dice only
```

In game code (T7's compiler, T8's player maps), with no level builder:

```gdscript
var parts := LevelParts.new()
parts.seed = map_hash
parts.width = map_w
parts.height = map_h
var made := KitPiece.build(spec, parts)
if made.has("error"):
	refuse(made["error"])  # one plain line
# parts.cells (open, floor, light, area), parts.geometry() (slabs, segments,
# ceilings, ladders), parts.props, parts.glows, parts.stations, parts.pieces
```

`tools/levels/geo_kit.gd kit_apply()` shows how the parts go into a level
(the cells are opened with their roof 0: the geometry is the roof).

What a piece says about itself (`parts.pieces[i]`, also what `build`
returns): `id`, `piece`, `footprint` [x0, y0, x1, y1], `front` (its front's
side on the map), `building`, `doors` [{id, at, face, z, width, head,
hung}], `floors` [{id, at, z}], `roof` {kind, at, z, reach}, `walk_in`
[{door, from, to}] and `reach` [{id, at}] (the kit check's test points),
`cost`, and `set_piece` (barn, tower) where it has one.

The same spec list in the same order gives the same parts byte for byte
(`parts.hash_text()`), on every machine: no clock, no play dice; looks dice
come from the piece's id and `parts.seed`; every number is snapped to a
millimetre.

## Refusals (one line, nothing of the piece is built)

| what | the line |
| --- | --- |
| no such piece | `no kit piece named mansion` |
| no id | `farmhouse: a kit piece needs an id` |
| a bad id | `farmhouse: an id is letters, digits and _ (a b)` |
| an id used twice | `barn: that id is taken` |
| an option the piece does not take | `home: farmhouse takes no stories` |
| a number out of range | `home: storeys is 1 to 3` |
| not whole | `home: storeys is a whole number` |
| the wrong kind | `home: w is a number`, `home: chimney is true or false`, `home: roof is one of gable, hip` |
| no such style | `home: no style named pink` |
| off the map (inside its border) | `home: runs off the map (30, 0 to 39, 9)` |
| on another building | `home: stands on barn (move one of them)` |
| too much in one piece | `home: 400 pieces of building, 320 most` |

## Limits (said plainly)

- Sizes and options are the ranges above; footprints are whole cells and
  turns are quarter turns.
- Doorways sit in the middle of cells (the nav graph walks cell to cell),
  and a round building's doorways are 1.6 wide so one of the cells lines up.
- A gambrel's lower slopes, a silo's cone and a steeple are faces nobody
  stands on; a pitch over 1 is not walked on.
- Doors are open doorways (T4 hangs doors); windows are open holes (T4 adds
  panes).
- A curved wall's pieces are straight (16 or more round a circle).
- The barn and water tower are marked as set pieces but do not come down
  yet (T6 joins them to `meta.set_pieces`).
- Furniture is geometry (blocks): pews, counters, bales; props are only the
  pumps, an ice chest, tyres, gas cans and the bell rope.

## New tiles (data/index.json, at the end)

`kit_shingle`, `kit_clapboard`, `kit_clapboard_grey`, `kit_trim`,
`kit_stone`, `kit_church_glass`, `kit_church_glass_lit` (glows),
`kit_staves`, `kit_canopy` (its light boxes glow), `kit_porch`, `kit_hay`.
Made by `tools/art/build_kit_art.gd`. The church glass has no broken
(`_brk`) or holed (`_bst`) copies: those are for windows in grid walls, and
the kit's windows are holes in geometry walls (T4's panes break).

## The check

`tools/kit_check.gd` (in check_all and its quick set, about 15 s): every
piece at its smallest and largest size in all 4 turns, mirrored and not,
compiles, stays on its footprint, is walked into through every doorway (the
nav graph from the yard; a body in two of the frames), has every floor and
roof meant to be reached reached, gives the same parts twice and keeps to
its cost; the plain house and the farmhouse in detail (sight through
windows and walls, up the stairs, up the ladder, over the ridge, out onto
the porch roof, the mesh); the refusals; and `town=36` times the town
stress_bench and cpu_check play. `write=<dir>` writes two gallery levels and
a picture tour of every piece for `tools/map/map_pictures.gd`.

## For the other tracks

- **T4:** doorways are in each piece's `doors` (`hung: false`, `z`, `width`,
  `head`, `face`); a barn's big doorways are 3 wide (span 3 slides), the
  store's and gas station's fronts 2 (a pair). Windows are holes: their
  openings are in the wall pieces' gaps (sill and head over each floor).
- **T6:** the barn and water tower say `set_piece`; the porch, trailer, store,
  gas station and church place glow pools (`parts.glows`); the church's lit
  glass and the canopy's light boxes glow by tile.
- **T7:** a source object `{"type": "farmhouse", "id": ..., "at": [x, y]}` is
  a kit spec (`type` is read as `piece`; turn `at` into `x`, `y`). The
  schema can be generated from `KitPiece.names()` and `KitPiece.catalog()`.
- **T8:** compile pieces with `KitPiece.build` into one `LevelParts`, check
  `parts.cost()` against the map budget, and copy the parts as
  `geo_kit.kit_apply` does.
- **T10:** `docs/map_catalog` should get a thumbnail per piece (the kit
  check's galleries are ready-made).
- **Engine fix in this track:** `FlowField._walk()` stopped its first walk
  after as many nodes as the map has cells; a graph with many decks has
  more nodes than that, so upper floors of a big kit map came out
  unreachable. It now walks every node (no change on any chapter: their
  walks always finished well inside the old bound).
