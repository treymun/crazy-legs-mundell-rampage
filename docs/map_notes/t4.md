# T4 WAYS: notes for the guide (pass 16)

What a level can say since pass 16 about the ways through it: doors up on a
storey and doors wider than a cell, glass in the geometry's walls, via points
for the nav graph, and the automap by floor. For T10 to fold into
MAP_AUTHORING.md. Plans write it through `tools/levels/geo_kit.gd` (and
`level_builder.gd`'s `door()`); the level file keys are below. Everything is
opt-in: a level that uses none of it is written, hashed and played exactly as
before. The test level is `tools/ways_check.gd`'s `level()`: read it as a
worked example (a two-storey house and a barn, about 60 lines).

## Turn the ways on first: `Geo.ways(b)`

Call it before the walls and stairs. From then on:

- every doorway a `Geo.wall()` cuts (an opening whose sill is within a step
  of the wall's foot, with no pane) is a **via**: its middle point;
- every `Geo.stairs()` gets two vias, the middle of its foot and of its head.

The level file then holds `"nav_via": true` and `"vias": [[x, y, z], ...]` in
its `geometry` block. **Every map an outside AI makes should call it**: it
costs nothing where the cells already line up.

```gdscript
Geo.ways(b)                                                # first
Geo.wall(b, "south", Vector2(4, 12), Vector2(14, 12), 0.0, 2.6,
	{"openings": [{"at": 3.0, "width": .8}]})              # a via at (7, 12)
Geo.stairs(b, "stair", [12, 5, 13, 11], 0.0, 2.6, "n")     # vias at its foot and head
Geo.via(b, 20.25, 8.0, 0.0)                                # one by hand
```

## Via points (the nav graph through off-grid ways)

Hands, the hound and every bot walk a graph of places: one at the middle of
each cell (and one on each deck over it). Two neighbouring places are joined
when a body walks straight from middle to middle. A doorway .8 wide whose
middle sits on a cell's corner misses every middle, so before pass 16 nobody
but the player came through it.

With `nav_via`, two neighbouring places (8 ways round, on floors within 1.0
of the via's) that are not joined yet are joined when a body walks from one
middle to the via and on to the other, both ways, and the via lies within .75
of the straight way between them (an opening between them, never a detour).
Bodies steer at the via until they are past it (`NavGraph.toward`):
`FlowField.heading()`, so hands, the hound, the campaign bot, the 5v5 bots
and the deathmatch bot all use it.

```json
"geometry": {"version": 1, "nav_via": true,
  "vias": [[7.0, 12.0, 0.0], [12.5, 11.0, 0.0], [12.5, 5.0, 2.6]], "slabs": [...]}
```

A via is `[x, y]` or `[x, y, z]`, z the floor there (0 when left out).

**Rules that keep stairs and doorways working:**
- A stair's treads must cover the middles of the cells it runs through (a
  stair one cell wide on whole cells, `[12, 5, 13, 11]`): each cell's place
  stands on its tread. A stair narrower than a cell, off the cells' middles,
  has no places on it; the vias at its ends cannot help that.
- Leave a stairwell open in the floor over it (decks round it, not over it).
- A window (a sill over a step) is never a way.

## Doors up on a storey and doors wider than a cell

A door stands across cell (x, y): its leaf on the line through the cell's
middle (`y + .5` for a door across x, `x + .5` for a `vertical` one), so the
wall it stands in must run along that line, with an opening the door's width.

| key | what | default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `z` | the floor the door stands on (an upper storey's), 0 to 100 | 0 (the ground) |
| `span` | cells wide along its wall: 1, 2 or 3 | 1 |
| `style` | `hinge` or `slide` | `hinge` from geo_kit, `slide` in a level file |

- `span` 2 or 3 with `hinge`: a pair of leaves, each half the span, hinged at
  the ends (a double door).
- `span` 2 or 3 with `slide`: one leaf the span long that slides aside along
  the wall's face (a barn door). It runs past the doorway's end, so leave the
  wall beside it free for the length of the span.
- Keys (`key`), bars (`bar`: n, s, e, w), `secret`, `id` and `opened` work as
  for any door. USE opens an upper door only from its own floor (within 1.2),
  and a wide door from anywhere along it. A closing door waits for a body in
  its doorway on its floor, not for one under it.
- An upper door changes nothing of the grid: the cells under it stay the room
  below (walk under it on the ground). Its walls are geometry.

In a plan (the wall first, then the door in its opening):

```gdscript
Geo.wall(b, "loft_mid", Vector2(4, 8.5), Vector2(14, 8.5), 2.6, 5.2,
	{"openings": [{"at": 4.5, "width": 1.0}]})             # x 8..9, sill from 2.6
Geo.door(b, 8, 8, false, {"z": 2.6, "id": "loft_door"})    # the loft door
Geo.door(b, 27, 4, false, {"span": 2})                     # a double door, cells 27 and 28
Geo.door(b, 24, 12, false, {"span": 3, "style": "slide"})  # a barn door, cells 24 to 26
```

`Geo.door` adds the door only (no grid change). On the grid (a door in a
wall cell), `b.door(x, y, vertical, {"span": 2})` opens all its cells.

A wall standing on an upper floor (`z0` over 0) cuts its doorways from that
floor now: an opening with no `sill` starts at `z0` and tops out 1.85 over it.
Before pass 16 such an opening was left solid (no chapter had one).

In the level file:

```json
{"x": 8, "y": 8, "style": "hinge", "vertical": false, "z": 2.6, "id": "loft_door", ...}
{"x": 24, "y": 12, "style": "slide", "vertical": false, "span": 3, ...}
```

## Triggers: close and lock

```json
"do": [{"close": [8, 8]}]          shut the door in that cell (any cell of a wide one)
"do": [{"lock": "loft_door"}]      shut it and hold it (USE: a remote switch's)
"do": [{"open": "loft_door"}]      open it (by id now too), and let a lock go
```

A lock is held again when a save is loaded (the fired triggers are saved).
Triggers do not run in versus modes (as ever).

## Glass in the geometry's walls (panes)

An opening with `"pane": true` gets glass from its sill to its head. Whole, it
stops bodies, shots and looks (as the grid's panes always have); a shot or a
blast within 2 breaks it (the glass burst and sound of a pane), and then it is
out of the way and not drawn. Broken panes are carried to clients in the
snapshot and kept in saves, as the grid's are.

```gdscript
Geo.wall(b, "east", Vector2(14, 4), Vector2(14, 12), 0.0, 2.6,
	{"openings": [{"at": 3.5, "width": 1.2, "sill": .9, "head": 1.9, "pane": true}]})
```

The level file's `"geo_panes": [{"a": [14, 6.9], "b": [14, 8.1], "z0": .9, "z1": 1.9}]`.
A pane down to the floor (a glass door) stops bodies but is not a way for the
nav graph.

## The automap by floor

Standing more than 1.2 over the cell's ground (on a deck: an upper storey,
a loft), the map shows that storey: the cells a walked-on floor within .6 of
the player's covers, their edges as walls, the geometry's walls standing on
it, the doors on it, and FLOOR 2 (3, ...) in its corner. Back on the ground
it shows the ground as ever. A map made with `Geo.ways` also draws its thin
walls on the ground's map.

## The bots on maps of storeys

- 5v5 (`TacticalNav`): a goal on a deck has a field of its own (its floor's
  place). A route point may be `[x, y, z]` and a hold `[x, y, facing, z]`; a
  goal where a player stands takes their floor. Without a z a goal is on the
  ground, as ever.
- Deathmatch and co-op (`tools/mode_bot.gd`): hunts walk the target's field
  from the bot's own floor, so they take the stairs.

## Refusals (one line each; the piece is left out or left plain)

| what | the line |
| --- | --- |
| a door's span not 1, 2 or 3 | `level <id> door x,y: span is 1, 2 or 3 cells` (one cell wide) |
| a door's z under 0 or over 100 | `... z is 0 to 100` (on the ground) |
| z or span not numbers | `... z and span are numbers` |
| a geo pane not `{a, b, z0, z1}`, shorter than .05 or longer than 16, z1 not over z0 | `level <id>: a geo pane is {a: [x, y], b: [x, y], z0, z1}` |
| vias not a list | `geometry: vias is a list of [x, y, z]` |
| a via not 2 or 3 numbers | `geometry: a via is [x, y] or [x, y, z]` |
| a via off the map | `geometry: the via at x, y is off the map` |
| over 4096 vias | `geometry: N vias, at most 4096 are read` |

## Limits that stay (said plainly)

- A door's leaf stands on a cell's middle line; a door in an angled wall, or
  on a cell's edge, is not possible (an opening there with no door is: a via
  finds it).
- A door is 1.85 high; up to 3 cells wide.
- The nav graph's places are the cells' middles: a way narrower than a body
  (.4) or a stair off the middles is no way for hands and bots.
- Glass stops a look, as the grid's panes do (hands do not see through
  windows until they are broken).
- The automap tells storeys by the walked-on floors' heights (within .6); a
  storey's rooms under one floor height show together.

## For the other tracks

- **T5 KIT:** call `Geo.ways(b)` in the kit's buildings (or write `nav_via`
  and `vias` yourself); doors in kit walls go through `Geo.door` (z, span);
  windows with glass are openings with `"pane": true`.
- **T7 SOURCE v2:** every v2 source should compile with `Geo.ways(b)` first;
  a source `door` takes `z` and `span`; an opening takes `pane`; a source
  `via` is `Geo.via`. The trigger verbs `close` and `lock` take a door's id.
- **T8 player maps:** write `nav_via` in every v2 player map's geometry; the
  refusals above are the loader's.
- **T10:** the guide's "Limitations" lines on doors (one cell, ground only),
  glass (grid only) and the nav graph (middles only) are lifted as above.
